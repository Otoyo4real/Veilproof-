Prove an agent is good for it — without handing over its wallet

Agents are starting to pay each other: compute, invoices, OTC deals. Before a deal, the other side wants proof the agent can pay — but publishing a treasury wallet invites targeting, scams and front-running. VeilProof changes the deal. An agent proves its treasury across every wallet it holds, proves the balance was HELD and not just deposited an hour ago, and carries a portable credit score any app can recompute from the same public rules. The facts come from the chain itself, so they cannot be faked. Only hashes of the wallets are ever published; the addresses go only to the verifier the agent chose, in a private package.

How it works

Three steps. The prover never reveals the wallet to the public; the verifier never has to trust the prover.

1 Prove Connect the wallet, sign an ownership challenge, and fetch its onchain snapshot straight from NEAR. Claims are evaluated in the browser.
2 Publish NEAR AI Cloud analyses the claims in a TEE and attests the result. Only the report hashes go onchain — never the address, never amounts.
3 Verify The verifier re-fetches the chain, re-runs every claim, and gets a clear VALID or INVALID — in one click, with no account and no wallet.

What it proves — and what it does not

The honest trade-off, stated plainly. VeilProof is a hybrid privacy model: the public sees only hashes, the chosen verifier sees the address because they must re-check the chain, and the AI sees claim aggregates only — never the address, never transaction hashes.

ClaimWhat proves it
The data is genuineThe verifier re-fetches it from the chain. Onchain data cannot be typed in by the user.
The balance was held, not flashedSampled balance history at past block heights: the lowest sampled balance over the window, not a snapshot.
The wallets are all the prover'sEach linked wallet proves ownership with a transfer to the primary wallet, or by signing a link message with its key — no transfer, no gas.
The credit score is realThe score is deterministic (score-v2, published rules): the verifier recomputes it from the same snapshots and the same windows. A tampered score fails verification.
The underwriting opinion is honestNEAR AI Cloud TEE attestation, nonce-bound to the request. The verdict is advisory — a thin score can never support a lend, by a deterministic guard.
The report was not alteredSHA-256 report hash anchored on NEAR; tampering fails verification.
The wallet belongs to the proverThe prover signs a challenge with the wallet key; the publish transaction records the signer as owner.
The wallet address stays privateOnly a hash commitment is published. The address travels only in the private disclosure package to the verifier the prover chose.
Anything offchainNothing. VeilProof only ever makes claims about onchain data.

Who uses it

Agent treasuries An agent proves it can settle a deal — across every wallet it holds, with a sustained balance and a portable score — without publishing the treasury addresses that would invite targeting.
Agent-to-agent credit A service agent checks a client agent's score and an attested underwriting opinion before extending compute or credit — the score is recomputable, so no one has to trust the claimant.
Lending A borrower proves balance, sustained history and a clean record to a lender without handing over the wallet that holds everything.
Grants and OTC deals A grantee proves onchain activity to a committee, or a counterparty proves it can settle before a deal is struck — the proof, not the address, changes hands.

Prove a wallet

The full prover flow, right here. Preview any wallet with no wallet connected; connect only to sign and publish.

Your wallet stays yours. Everything on this page runs in your browser. The chain is read, never written to, until you choose to publish — and publishing records only hashes, never your address, never amounts, never transactions. The optional AI analysis sees claim-level facts only — never your address, never transaction hashes. A report without an AI summary says "not analysed", never "verified by AI".
1

Preview a wallet — no wallet needed

Paste any NEAR address and see its claims. The chain is public, so everything in steps 1–4 runs in your browser with no wallet connected. You only connect a wallet to sign the challenge and publish — and it must be the wallet that owns the address you previewed. A preview alone proves nothing about who you are; the signature is the proof.

A bare hash is assumed to be yours; use hash,senderId for incoming transfers sent by someone else. Hashes that cannot be verified onchain are skipped.

Linking more wallets aggregates their balances, volume and activity into one report — an agent's treasury is usually spread across wallets. Each linked wallet proves ownership in one of two ways: a small transfer to the primary address (below), or — no transfer, no gas — by signing a short link statement. Leave the hash blank for a wallet you will link by signature instead.

Sampling reads the balance at past block heights, so the report can prove a balance was HELD, not just deposited an hour ago. The account must be older than the window you pick.

2

Connect your wallet to sign

Connect the wallet that owns the address you previewed, then sign a short ownership challenge. The signature is what a lender trusts — only the owner of the key could have made it. Publishing in step 5 is a transaction from the same wallet.

Not connected. Meteor and MyNearWallet are supported.

3

Choose the policy, evaluate the claims

Leave a field blank to skip that claim. Thresholds are in NEAR. Windows are in days. A minimum tx count or volume needs its window.

Aggregate claims sum across every linked wallet (a transfer between two of your own wallets is counted once). Sustained and time-weighted claims need balance sampling above.

4

Disclose, analyse, build the report

Choose which evaluated claims go into the public report. Claims you leave out are simply absent from the report — the verifier never sees them unless you disclose them another way.

The underwriting judgment is advisory: NEAR AI Cloud reads the deterministic score and claim facts (never your address) and gives a lend / lend with limits / decline verdict with a suggested limit. The score itself is the verifiable fact — any app can recompute it from the disclosure package.

AI analysis not run yet. The report will say "not analysed" unless you run it.

5

Publish the hashes onchain

Publishing is a real testnet transaction, signed by your wallet. Only the three hashes and the report id go onchain — never your address, never amounts, never transactions. The contract charges storage at cost and refunds any excess deposit.
